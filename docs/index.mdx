title: "Vexa Delivery"
---

Vexa Delivery keeps a self-hosted [Vexa](https://github.com/Vexa-ai/vexa) deployment always
up to date through a signed channel your cluster pulls from. Nothing reaches in: every image
is digest-pinned and signed, verified inside your perimeter against keys you pin, and your
production moves only when you move the pin.

There are two ways to consume a channel. Same machinery, same kit, same verification — the
difference is what travels through it and the obligations attached.

## The open channel — free

Carries Vexa's open-source releases exactly as published: Apache-2.0 images and charts,
digest-pinned and signed. Subscribe your cluster and it stays current with upstream —
staging rolls automatically, you promote to production under your own attestation.
Community support.

## The enterprise channel — subscription

A private channel operated for your company. Releases arrive attestation-complete, with the
full evidence set — gate reports and station verdicts from real environments — and no
release reaches your channel until it has been rendered with your values and matched against
the contract agreed for your company. A support lane with response commitments sits behind
it.

The software is identical on both paths, and all of it is open source. The subscription
buys the operated stream and the obligations attached to it — evidence, contract gating,
support — not software features.

## Three moves

1. **Set up a generic environment** — a Kubernetes cluster, an OpenShift namespace, a managed
   cloud. Nothing Vexa-specific.
2. **Set up the channel** — one pull-only credential, one subscription.
3. **The software stays up to date** — inside your perimeter, on your schedule.
