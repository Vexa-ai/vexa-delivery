---
title: "Telemetry ladder"
description: "What comes back, at which rung, on whose authority — and why content is on no rung at all."
---

You want the better product. You hold a security policy. Those are not in tension if the
choice is yours: **we offer the full spectrum of callback kinds and you pick the rung.**
Policy is yours, mechanics are ours, one host in both directions.

Every rung is the same mechanism: a **bundle of plain, inspectable files** pushed to your
station's write path on the channel host you already pull from. No streams, no second
endpoint, no new firewall rule. Your security team can open any bundle before it leaves —
and the tool prints its path and stops before sending, precisely so they can.

## The rungs

| Tier | What comes back | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **T0 — silent** | nothing. Entries can even arrive by sneakernet. | — |
| **T1 — receipts** | signed install and validation verdicts: which pin, which entry, sync and health words, the PreSync verifier's verdict, the image digests actually running | per release |
| **T2 — health** | aggregate counters: pod phase counts, restart counts, deployment ready ratios, CronJob last-success ages, node readiness. **No identities, no content, no logs.** | daily (or weekly) |
| **T3 — usage** | activation and volume aggregates, pseudonymous: activated-user counts, meeting and minute volumes, tokens per model | daily or weekly |
| **T4 — diagnostics** | scrubbed log and trace bundles. **Never automatic** — exported by your admin, read by you, handed over per incident | on demand |

<Warning>
**Content is not on the ladder at any rung.** Transcripts and meeting bodies never leave.
This is the fixed point that makes every other rung negotiable, and it is enforced by the
shape of the document rather than by our restraint: [`report.v1`](https://github.com/Vexa-ai/vexa-delivery/blob/main/spec/report.v1.schema.json)
sets `additionalProperties: false` on **every** object, so there is no field a transcript
could go in. A payload carrying one fails validation on your side, before it is sent.
</Warning>

## Enforcement is structural, and it runs in both directions

Your `contract.yaml` declares the rung. Three independent things then hold it:

**1 · The packager cannot collect above it.** `collectors.collect()` resolves your declared
tier to a list of collector functions **before calling any of them**. At T2 the usage
collector is not called and its output discarded — the function is never referenced. That
distinction is the whole point: *"we filter the output afterwards"* is a promise, and
*"the code that reads it is not reachable"* is a property you can check by reading
[`kit/validate/collectors.py`](https://github.com/Vexa-ai/vexa-delivery/blob/main/kit/validate/collectors.py).

**2 · The schema refuses a payload that exceeds its own declared tier**, so even a bug in
(1) cannot produce one.

**3 · Our ingest refuses a bundle exceeding your declared scope.** A T3-shaped bundle
arriving against a T2 contract is refused with a named error (`S10`) and nothing is kept.

That third one is the half worth anything. You can read the packager yourself. You cannot
read what happens on our machine — so that is where the check has to be, and it is us
enforcing your policy against ourselves.

## Declaring your rung

In your station's `contract.yaml`, inside the `report_scope` block you already have:

```yaml
report_scope:
  schema: report.v1
  tier: 2                          # 0-4. Your declaration; nothing else may set it.
  cadence: daily                   # per-release · daily · weekly · on-demand
  trigger: explicit-command-only   # or `scheduled` — see below
  destination: channel.vexa.ai
  allowed_files: [station.json, contract.yaml, ...]
  require_redaction_verified: true
```

**`tier` has exactly one authority and this is it.** It is deliberately not settable from a
values file: a second place to write it is a second place it can disagree with the first,
and the disagreement would be silent.

<Note>
**A `report_scope` with no `tier` is tier 1.** Every contract written before the ladder
existed has that shape, and every one of them already permits exactly the operator's
install bundle — which is what T1 is. A contract with **no `report_scope` at all** is a
refusal at both ends: without it there is no bound to enforce, and we do not pick one for
you.
</Note>

## The trigger, and what did not change

`explicit-command-only` is still the default and still means what it always meant: nothing
in the delivered software originates a report. It happens when an operator types
`--submit`.

`scheduled` exists because T2 and T3 are **cadenced** rungs, and a rung nobody reaches
without a human typing a command every morning is a rung nobody climbs. Setting it is what
authorises the in-cluster CronJob — **the station chart renders no CronJob unless your
contract says `scheduled`.** The timer is authorised by the same document that bounds what
it may send, and no timer ever appears in software you have already installed.

## Sending, per rung

**T1 — per release.** A PostSync hook Job fires when a sync completes, which is exactly
when *what is running here, under what verdict* changes. It is not a timer: it fires on an
act you performed.

**T2 / T3 — cadenced.** Either the CronJob (if you declared `scheduled`), or by hand:

```bash
vexa-validate --report --namespace vexa-prod \
  --contract ./contract.yaml --station <your-station> \
  --submit --submit-dry-run          # prints the payload path; sends nothing
```

Drop `--submit-dry-run` to send. The RBAC the sender holds is `get`/`list` on pods,
deployments, cronjobs and jobs, plus `get` on one named ConfigMap — no secrets, no exec, no
logs, no write.

**T4 — on demand, and it does not send.**

```bash
vexa-validate --export-diagnostics --namespace vexa-prod --out ./diag
```

Writes a scrubbed bundle to your disk and stops. There is no `--submit` path for tier 4 and
there is no `tier: 4` value in `report.v1` — a schema that could express T4 is a schema that
could carry one automatically. Secret and ConfigMap values, container env values and
credential-shaped annotations are removed; object names, images, conditions and event
messages are kept, because without them the bundle diagnoses nothing. **Read it, then share
it however your policy allows.** The reason the command exists is that *"send us your
logs"* otherwise means an engineer improvising `kubectl logs` under incident pressure, which
is exactly when redaction gets skipped.

## Absent over faked

A counter that could not be collected is reported **absent, with a reason** — never
defaulted to zero. Zero is a claim. A CronJob that has never succeeded reports `null`, not
`0`, because *never* and *a moment ago* are different facts and rendering them the same is
how a dashboard shows green on a dead job.

**T3 ships today as an interface with no implementation, and says so.** Nothing in this
software can produce activation or volume counts yet — the product's usage surface is not
exposed to a station-side collector. So the collector returns the block with every counter
null and one `absent` row explaining why. Inventing plausible numbers would put fabricated
data into your ledger under our signature, in a document whose entire value is that it is
inspectable.

## What climbing buys you

| Rung | What it buys |
|---|---|
| **T1** | support entitlement — we can answer *"which bytes are they on?"* without asking you |
| **T2** | we see your degradation before you file a ticket |
| **T3** | benchmarks against the fleet, tuned defaults, capacity planning |

An exchange rate priced in your own policy currency. Nothing above your declared rung
leaves, and nothing above it is kept.
