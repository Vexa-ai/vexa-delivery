**A release is built once, signed once, and admitted only if your policy says so.**

Each release reaches you as one OCI artifact:

- **`entry.json`** — signed record naming the version, source commit, image digests (`@sha256:…`) and the sha256 of every evidence file.
- **`evidence/`** — digest map, delivery receipt from Vexa's own production run, SLSA provenance, and the Sigstore trust material to verify offline.
- **`VERIFY.md`** — the commands with this release's file names filled in.
- Beside it, the Helm chart as an OCI artifact, every digest baked into its values — including images the runtime spawns on demand.

Two fields are contractual: **`evidence_absent`** names evidence this release lacks; **`break_glass`** names who authorised an incomplete chain, and why.

Your **staging** follows the channel pointer. Your **production** follows a pin only you move.

## The loop

1. **Publish** — images freeze and are signed; Vexa staging and production add evidence.
2. **Gate** — rendered with **your** values, against **your** contract. See [the station gate](station-gate).
3. **Channel** — the gated entry lands; the `current` pointer moves.
4. **Pull** — your Argo CD polls over your pull-only credential, or your mirror.
5. **Admission** — Kyverno checks the signature, refuses anything not digest-pinned.
6. **Smoke** — a human watches the bot capture a real meeting. Dated receipt.
7. **Station bundle** — receipts, redacted values, profile, contract; sent back by hand.
8. **Back to 2** — that bundle is the station the next release is gated against.

<Note>
**Rung.** Step 6 is the intended procedure and the smoke tool implements it, but **no audio has reached the pipeline in any delivery run yet** — [what's proven, and where](tested#the-delivered-software).
</Note>

Vexa and the machinery that receives it both run in your cluster; the machinery is one signed chart, pinned in the subscription `install.sh` applies, and a hand-edit to either pin is reverted visibly.

## Channels — open or private

Everything Vexa runs on is open source and publicly published — images on Docker Hub, charts and code in the open repositories.

- **The open channel** delivers those public releases exactly as published — digest-pinned, signed, pulled from Docker Hub — so a self-hosted deployment stays current automatically. Community support.
- **Your private channel** is operated with us for your company: releases arrive attestation-complete with the full evidence set (gate reports, station verdicts from real environments), gated against your own specification before anything reaches you, with a support lane behind it.

The channel is two-way, and the return path differs. On the open channel, the way back is GitHub issues — plus any station report you choose to share. On your private channel, the return path is part of the contract: a ticket lane with response commitments, and the [telemetry rung you set](telemetry-ladder) — from silent to receipts to health counters to diagnostics, never content. The more your side chooses to send back, the faster your deployment improves — that bandwidth is what your private channel is for.

The software is identical on both paths, and switching paths is a subscription change, not a migration.
