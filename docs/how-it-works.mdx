---
title: "How it works"
description: "We publish, your cluster pulls and checks, a bad release is refused before it syncs, a receipt returns."
---

1. **We publish** a release to a channel, with its validation evidence.
2. **Your cluster pulls it.** Argo CD syncs; Kyverno verifies the signatures with cosign at admission, and — when you install with [`--verifier-image`](install#step-3-install), which is what turns that gate on — the PreSync verifier checks the entry against your contract before the sync. That is what makes the update safe to roll.
3. **A release that does not pass is refused before it syncs**, rather than rolled back after. Going back is one command and it is yours: production follows a pin you move, and moving it to the previous version is [Roll back](operations#roll-back). Nothing here reverts a release on its own — staging's `selfHeal` reverts hand-drift, not a bad release, and production has no automated sync at all.
4. **A receipt goes back to the channel** when you send it, so we know how it went.

<img src="/images/delivery-loop.svg" alt="Vexa publishes and gates a release into the channel; your cluster pulls it, admits it, deploys to staging, a human smokes it, you promote to production, and a station report returns to the channel" style={{width:"100%",maxWidth:"1070px"}} />

Argo CD works against a generic, deterministic state — a Kubernetes namespace — so this is a deterministic operation. Nothing is pushed to your cluster.

We have no access to your cluster. Your cluster has access to the channel, one way. The loop closes with a receipt, and that is the only thing flowing back.
