The channel keeps the deployment inside your perimeter current and within your agreed
specification. A release is built once: a set of images identified by content digest
(`@sha256:…`), never rebuilt. It reaches you on a private channel with its evidence attached,
and your cluster admits it only if your policy says so.

## The channel entry

Each release arrives as one OCI artifact: **`entry.json`**, the signed record naming the
version, source commit, digest set and the sha256 of every evidence file; **`evidence/`**,
holding the digest map, the delivery receipt from Vexa's own production run, SLSA provenance
and the Sigstore trust material to verify offline; and **`VERIFY.md`**, the commands with this
release's file names filled in.

Beside it sits the Helm chart as an OCI artifact with every image digest baked into its
values, including images the runtime spawns on demand. Your Argo CD applies that chart.

Two fields are contractual: **`evidence_absent`** names evidence this release lacks;
**`break_glass`** names who authorised an incomplete chain, and why.

## Your two environments

**Staging** follows the channel pointer: pulled, verified at admission, applied.
**Production** follows a **pin** — nothing arrives until you move it, and Vexa cannot. The
subscription is one ApplicationSet plus two policies, on a channel that is private always:
shared by estate shape, or dedicated where a customer's own contract gates its releases. See
[the channel](channel).

Every node above is the same box. Each stage that validates a release signs it — Vexa staging
its validation plus a human witness, Vexa production its real usage soak, your staging your
own checks. The artifact never changes; a candidate that fails stops accumulating evidence.

## The station bundle

Two things run in your cluster: Vexa, and the machinery that receives Vexa. That machinery —
subscription, contract, admission rules, floor check — is one signed chart pinned by your
bootstrap file. Releases move by the release pin, machinery by the bundle pin; a hand-edit to
either is reverted visibly.

## The loop

1. **Publish** — images freeze and are signed; Vexa staging and production add evidence.
2. **Gate** — rendered with **your** values, against **your** contract. See
   [the station gate](station-gate).
3. **Channel** — the gated entry lands; the `current` pointer moves.
4. **Pull** — your Argo CD polls over your pull-only credential, or your mirror.
5. **Admission** — Kyverno checks the signature, refuses anything not digest-pinned.
6. **Smoke** — a human watches the bot capture a real meeting. Dated receipt.
7. **Station bundle** — receipts, redacted values, profile, contract; sent back by hand.
8. **Back to 2** — that bundle is the station the next release is gated against.

**The standards underneath:** build-once / promote-by-digest over OCI content addressing ·
Sigstore signing · SLSA Provenance v1 · in-toto attestations · Binary-Authorization-style
admission enforced by Kyverno · GitOps reconciliation by Argo CD.
