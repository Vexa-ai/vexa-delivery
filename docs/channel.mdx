title: "The channel"
description: "Where your releases live, who may pull them, and how your credential arrives."

The channel is what keeps your deployment current: a private OCI repository on
**`channel.vexa.ai`**, a registry Vexa operates. It
carries signed entries, the digest-pinned Helm chart, image signatures, the kit, and a
`current` pointer. Your staging follows `current`; your production follows a pin only you
move. Mirroring the whole channel into your own registry is first-class: the guarantees are in
the signatures and digests, not the transport.

## Shared by shape, dedicated by contract

| | **Shared channel** | **Dedicated channel** |
|---|---|---|
| Who is on it | Many subscribers of one estate **shape** — an OpenShift-bank shape, an AKS shape, generic `enterprise-stable` | One customer |
| What it carries | The standard enterprise bundle for that shape | Releases gated by that customer's contract |
| Credentials | Per subscriber, independently revocable | Per subscriber |

The default is the shared channel of your shape. Sharing one shares nothing else: your
credential reads your channel only, your values never leave your cluster, your pin is yours.

## Your credential

One **pull-only** account scoped to your channel, minted, rotated and revoked with
`vexa_subscriber.py list | add | revoke` (`add` also rotates; revocation lands within a
minute). The credential and the trust anchor travel by different routes:

| Artefact | How it reaches you |
|---|---|
| `channel.pub`, the cosign public key — not secret | Published, and read aloud on a call |
| `<account>:<password>` — secret | Encrypted with [`age`](https://github.com/FiloSottile/age) to a key you already control, usually an SSH public key from your GitHub account, fingerprint cited in the mail |

```bash
age -d -i ~/.ssh/id_ed25519 channel-credential.age
```

If your organization has its own vendor-credential intake, that is used instead. Rotation
invalidates nothing you already pulled.

## Using it

```bash
oras login channel.vexa.ai -u <your-account> --password-stdin
oras pull channel.vexa.ai/vexa/channel/<your-channel>:current -o entry/
```

Then [verify](verify) before anything runs.

## Freshness and revocation

<Note>
**Rung.** Both land with [PR #38](https://github.com/Vexa-ai/vexa-delivery/pull/38), open at
the time of writing.
</Note>

Entries **expire**. A channel nobody has published to goes stale, and your verifier says that
in its own words rather than in the vocabulary of a bad signature:

```text
OK    entry signature verifies against the pinned channel key
FAIL  STALE CHANNEL — this entry expired at 2026-08-24T12:19:45Z, 1 day(s) ago. The
      signature is not in question and nothing has been tampered with: nobody has
      published to this channel since. Contact Vexa.
```

The `OK` above the `FAIL` is the point — an expired entry and a forged one are different
incidents and must not read alike. Your pinned release keeps running. `max_entry_age_days` in
your contract tightens the horizon below ours; nothing widens it.

**Revocation** is a cosign-signed list on the channel. A release found defective is named on
it with a reason and a destination, and your verifier refuses it before the next sync:

```text
OK    revocation list signature verifies against the pinned channel key
  CRITICAL: … -> move to v0.12.24
FAIL  release v0.12.23 is REVOKED by the channel
```

No list published at all is an **empty** list, not an error — the other reading would refuse
every install made before the capability existed. **Your admission controller cannot read this
list:** revocation is enforced by the verify gate, not by Kyverno.

## Worth knowing

- **Pull-only is enforced at the edge proxy, not by the registry** — htpasswd cannot express
  read-without-write, so mutating verbs are gated in front of it and require the publisher
  credential.
- Bypassing that gate would still admit nothing: entries are signed with a key that is not on
  the registry, images are digest-pinned, and your policy checks both.
