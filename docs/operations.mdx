Your **staging** follows the channel pointer: a new entry is pulled, verified at admission
and applied on the next poll. Your **production** does nothing until you move its pin.

## Promote to production

When your staging soak satisfies your change process:

```bash
kubectl -n argocd patch applicationset vexa-channel-subscription --type=json \
  -p '[{"op":"replace","path":"/spec/generators/0/list/elements/1/position","value":"0.12.24"}]'
```

The pin is the release's chart version (`0.12.24`, no `v` prefix); staging holds `*`, the
newest entry on your channel. That is the whole promotion ceremony — one change, in your
cluster, in your window. Admission verification runs again in production regardless.

## Accept the upgrade

Same standard as a first install — a human watches it capture a real meeting:

```bash
python3 kit/smoke/vexa_smoke.py --namespace vexa-staging \
  --customer-values my-values.yaml --flows
```

The dated receipt names the chart revision and image digests, so "we accepted 0.12.24 on the
19th" is a file, not a memory. Run
[`vexa_validate.py`](install#step-5-send-the-station-bundle-back) instead to also refresh the
station bundle — an upgrade is when your station's shape is most likely to have moved.

## Roll back

The same field, the same patch, the previous version:

```bash
kubectl -n argocd patch applicationset vexa-channel-subscription --type=json \
  -p '[{"op":"replace","path":"/spec/generators/0/list/elements/1/position","value":"0.12.23"}]'
```

Argo rolls the workloads in reverse; admission verifies the older digests against the same
key. Entries never disappear and digests are immutable, so what you go back to is
byte-identical to what you ran.

**It rolls back code, not data.** One step back is safe because each release reads the
previous release's schema. Two steps back is a restore. **Check the migration notes** in the
release's evidence bundle first.

## Break-glass in your cluster

Your cluster, your admin, your audit log — there is no path from Vexa into it.

Staging syncs automatically and self-heals, so an out-of-band change there is reverted on the
next poll: suspend that Application's automated sync before you touch anything. Production has
no automated sync at all.

Declare it, suspend, act — then **reconverge through the gate**: resume the sync and let
admission verify what is now running. Drift is visible in your own Argo. A residue that
violates your contract is refused, not quietly baked in; you clear it by redoing the change as
a release, amending your values, or recording a waiver. There is no fourth path where the
cluster keeps running something nothing verified.

What reaches Vexa is a status-shaped report of what happened. Never a command.

## Update the kit

```bash
./vexa-kit/self-update.sh --check   # what would move
./vexa-kit/self-update.sh           # move it
```

Resolves `latest` to a digest, verifies it against your pinned key, swaps the tree
atomically. On a signature failure nothing is written and the running tree is untouched.

## If the channel goes away

Your pinned release keeps running. Argo CD reconciles against digests it has
already pulled, so an unreachable channel stops upgrades, not workloads.

On our side the channel's contents are a distribution copy: the entries,
charts and signatures it serves are rebuilt from tagged releases, and the
record of what was published — release, sequence, chart digest — is held in
git, replicated, and independent of the registry. A total loss of the channel
storage costs us hours of republishing and costs you nothing: no re-install,
no re-pinning, no re-verification. Signatures verify against the key you
pinned, which does not change.

## Also worth knowing

- **Drift** — an out-of-band change is reverted on the next sync, not merely reported. A
  permanent local difference belongs in your values.
- **Break-glass entries** — an entry with an incomplete evidence chain carries a `break_glass` record
  (person, reason, timestamp) your verifier sees before you decide. Refusing all break-glass
  entries is a supported policy.
- **Withdrawal** — a release found defective after publication is marked withdrawn and stops
  being promotable. Your pinned release keeps running; withdrawal never touches your cluster.
